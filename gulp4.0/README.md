## Part Two: Evolutionary Dynamics—Paradox Gradient and Conservation Laws

### 1. The Definition of Paradox Gradient

Once the Absolute Paradox breaks nothingness, evolution begins. But what determines the speed and direction of this evolution?

GULP proposes a dynamical law more fundamental than physical laws: **Paradox Gradient**.

> **Paradox Gradient = Intensity of Contradiction / Resolution Capacity of the Current Logical Structure**

- **Intensity of Contradiction**: The degree of difference in the internal states of the system. In physical systems, this manifests as energy potential differences, density differences, temperature differences, and tensions between quantum states; in social systems, as resource inequality and conflicts of interest; in psychological systems, as cognitive dissonance.
- **Resolution Capacity**: The maximum ability of the current logical structure to resolve contradictions per unit time. In physical systems, this manifests as energy dissipation rate and thermal conductivity; in living systems, as metabolic regulation capacity; in civilizations, as institutional resilience.

When the Paradox Gradient is low, the system is in a steady state—contradictions can be effectively managed by the existing structure. When the Paradox Gradient continuously rises, approaching and exceeding the resolution capacity of the existing structure, the system enters a critical state. At the critical point, the system faces two possibilities: either collapse, or leap to a higher level through structural reorganization—this is a **Logic Phase Transition**.

**Steady state** is the system's temporary compromise. It is not a state without contradictions, but a state where contradictions are effectively managed.

The essence of this phase transition is: the system changes from "passively gathering matter" to "actively releasing energy." This is not a quantitative change, but a fundamental restructuring of the organizational mode.
